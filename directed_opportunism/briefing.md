# Your strategy brief and the common map

For each level's own strategy brief and the briefs it gives its subordinates (START_TEXT, "Who holds what"). You write at your own level; you never write your superior's part. "First, a briefing is written down. This ensures that it has been heard" (p.171).

## `BRIEF.md`: the book's template (pp.249–250), its headings verbatim
1 CONTEXT: What is the situation?
2 HIGHER INTENT: One level up (my boss) / Two levels up (my boss’s boss). Quote the intent one and two levels above, where those levels exist, naming their sources; nothing further up (p.172). For the CEO's direct subordinate, one level up is `intent.md`; there is no level above the CEO [interp]. A brief you write for a subordinate also quotes the lexicon entries its task needs (p.62).
3 MY INTENT: What are we trying to achieve and why? What: … in order to … Why: … Measures. "An intent is the decision to do something now (a task) in order to achieve an outcome (a purpose)" (p.110). The superior one level up's what becomes your why (p.169); quote its source beside your own understanding.
4 IMPLIED TASKS: Main Tasks / Responsibility / Timing. "(Which task is the main effort? Highlight in bold or color)". "every level has to work out what its own main effort is" (p.115).
5 BOUNDARIES: Freedoms / Constraints. Quote inherited boundaries with their sources; each superior may set boundaries for its subordinates (p.191), within inherited direction, and labels them as its own [interp]. Add the control loop: "I can make the control loop tight or loose" (p.191): when you report to your superior, and what you refer back (p.174).
6 BACKBRIEF: HAS THE SITUATION CHANGED? "• No – our brief is valid • Yes – we have to change some tasks, but what we are trying to achieve is still valid • Yes – and we have to change what we are trying to achieve". Answer it each session against your brief and higher intent (p.202: "The main theme of the reviews is the question: 'Has the situation changed?'").

A field you have no source for stays empty.

## `MAP.md`: the common map (p.238)
The CEO's direct subordinate holds this project's map [interp]. Reports "create a common map of the situation, collective progress to date, current priorities and everybody's part in the plan" (p.238). Write those four as a story of the work so far, each part showing how it bears on the CEO's intent, and an "In progress" section. Facts a command can read from the ground (git, tests) stay out of it.
