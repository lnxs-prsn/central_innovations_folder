# Your strategy brief and the common map

For the CEO's direct subordinate in this project. You write both at your own level, from the CEO's intent in `intent.md`; you never write the CEO's part. "First, a briefing is written down. This ensures that it has been heard" (p.171).

## `BRIEF.md`: the book's template (pp.249–250), its headings verbatim
1 CONTEXT: What is the situation?
2 HIGHER INTENT: One level up (my boss) / Two levels up (my boss’s boss). Here: `intent.md`, quoted whole or pointed to; the CEO is the level above, and there is no level above the CEO [interp].
3 MY INTENT: What are we trying to achieve and why? What: … in order to … Why: … Measures. "An intent is the decision to do something now (a task) in order to achieve an outcome (a purpose)" (p.110). The why is the CEO's words, quoted.
4 IMPLIED TASKS: Main Tasks / Responsibility / Timing. "(Which task is the main effort? Highlight in bold or color)". "every level has to work out what its own main effort is" (p.115).
5 BOUNDARIES: Freedoms / Constraints. Only constraints the CEO stated (quoted) or that you set for your subordinates and label as yours; a constraint with no source is not one [evidence, directed_opportunism `evidence/terrain.md`: T18, T20]. Add the control loop: "I can make the control loop tight or loose" (p.191): when you report to the CEO, and what you refer back (p.174).
6 BACKBRIEF: HAS THE SITUATION CHANGED? "• No – our brief is valid • Yes – we have to change some tasks, but what we are trying to achieve is still valid • Yes – and we have to change what we are trying to achieve". Answer it each session against `intent.md` (p.202: "The main theme of the reviews is the question: 'Has the situation changed?'").

A field you have no source for stays empty.

## `MAP.md`: the common map (p.238)
Reports "create a common map of the situation, collective progress to date, current priorities and everybody's part in the plan" (p.238). Write those four as a story of the work so far, each part showing how it bears on the CEO's intent, and an "In progress" section. Facts a command can read from the ground (git, tests) stay out of it.
