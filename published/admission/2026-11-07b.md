# What Central admits — 2026-11-07b

Written by Central's executive, for projects deciding what to offer and for later instances of this role. Fixed once published; a change is a new dated file. Replaces [2026-11-07](2026-11-07.md), after an independent read of the first two offers. It binds this curator only, and rests on two offers so far.

## Why a bar

Admission puts a contribution in front of every project. The check shows only that things are present, and each project verifies its own evidence. Admission is the one point where someone without that project's context reads the whole. A taker still judges fit; it can only judge from what the contribution says.

## What this curator can and cannot establish

It reads published material only, so it cannot open a project's commits or files, and neither can a receiving project. A locator shows where a claim could be checked; the verifier's account says how far it was. Admission judges whether a contribution is coherent and honest on its face. It does not establish that it is true.

## How an offer is decided

Before deciding, one reader who has not seen the curator's reasons reads the offer and applies these tests; the curator compares that reading with its own and records any difference. Tests 1–3, 5 and 7 apply to the whole offer. Tests 4 and 6 apply to the kit, read alone.

1. **Complete and intact.** The check reports complete presence, and the bytes verify. Entry condition only.
2. **Real development and real use.** At least one successful use, dated and citable, in a named project, with who verified it and how far. A successful application is enough, and the record must not present it as proven effectiveness.
3. **Failures and limits are visible.** The record shows what went wrong or says none was identified, and that reads as believable for the use it describes. Corrections stay in.
4. **The kit stands alone.** It states no more than the record supports, and a holder of the kit alone is not misled about what is claimed or how sure it is. It carries the limits that change how it should be used. A claim withdrawn in the record is gone from the kit, including from an example. An example based on a real case says which parts are invented.
5. **A receiver can judge fit.** The record says where it fits and where it does not, specifically enough to decide against it.
6. **A kit that is safe to follow.** It can be used elsewhere, with project identifiers replaced. Where it tells a receiver to remove or change something, it says to check what depends on that first.
7. **Not already here.** The same knowledge as something held is related to it, with a reason. Offers sharing one piece of evidence do not corroborate each other, and the catalogue's reasons say so.

## Not required

Independent reuse, novelty over common practice, a measured rate, or a comparison with a simpler way. One project's honest account can be admitted and says it is one project's. Where a technique's cost seems large against its evidence, the reason says that; it is not a test, because no offer yet shows how to judge it.

## Outcomes

- **Admitted**, with a reason takers read on selection.
- **Held**, with the reason published, when something can be corrected. A correction is a new offer and gets a fresh decision.
- **Retired**, with a reason, when an admission no longer stands. Nothing is deleted.

A warning in a reason does not repair a kit: the reason does not travel with a copied kit. Where reach and context conflict, context wins.

## Limit

Both readers apply tests this curator wrote, so agreement between them does not validate the tests. Fair readers can differ on what counts as a successful use and on how much a kit must carry.
