## What the tests can still miss

1. **Control by the author-decider.** The author can choose the tests, the offered evidence, the input boundary, the reader and which differences to accept. The procedure requires recording differences but gives no independent appeal, conflict rule, or external check on that interpretation. An apparently clean independent read can therefore coexist with selective framing. The offer acknowledges the risk; passing these tests does not resolve it.
2. **Repeated runs and selection.** The method preserves the decider's first reading, but does not require disclosure of all reader runs, discarded reports, or why a particular reader was chosen. A decider could publish a favorable comparison after trying several readers. I saw no evidence of that here; the tests would not reliably expose it.
3. **Shared omissions and constrained evidence.** Two readers can agree because the tests omit a material receiver risk or the bundle excludes contrary evidence. Closed input protects the comparison from the decider's reasons but can also prevent discovery. Agreement, count of faults, and a changed decision do not measure what both missed.
4. **Net value and transfer.** There is no requirement to compare this procedure with a simpler check, to measure false alarms or bad holds, or to observe receiver outcomes. The record supports finding additional faults in this setting, not a demonstrated return on the reported cost elsewhere. The bar expressly leaves comparative effectiveness outside admission requirements.
5. **Follow-through.** Recording a difference or correcting a public statement does not by itself show that future decisions or copied kits improved. The tests admit this account without longitudinal verification. Receivers could mistake a successful review event for durable quality control if they disregard the stated limits.

These are residual risks, not additional admission tests I have silently imposed. In this offer they are mitigated by candid limits, not eliminated by the independent reading.
