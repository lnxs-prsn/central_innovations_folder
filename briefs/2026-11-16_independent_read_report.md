### 4. Kit stands alone — FAIL, with a judgment boundary

The kit does carry substantial context: “It rests on one use, in one project, on two offers”; “It did not change the outcome”; and “Agreement between the two readers does not validate the tests” (kit, preface). It also carries the different-provider qualification and approximate cost. It makes no general claim of proven effectiveness. Its bracketed tests are identified as “Example tests that served in the one use,” not an invented worked outcome; I found no disguised fictional case requiring a separate invention label.

However, the kit says “There it found five faults the deciding reader had missed or published as absent” and sends the holder to “the contribution's PATTERN.md.” Read alone, it never carries the record's limitation “The evidence was verified by the deciding role itself” (record, context record, limits). The independence of the second reading and the independence of verification of its claimed success are different matters. For a technique offered to counter a decider's own blind spots, the latter qualification materially affects how much confidence a receiver should place in this one success. A reference to a file the holder need not have does not carry it.

Correction: add to the kit's retained preface that the originating deciding role checked the evidence itself, what that check established, and that the successful-use account has not been independently verified. No new verification is required to make that disclosure.

A fair reader could pass this test: the kit does not explicitly claim independent verification, and its one-use and shared-tests caveats may suffice for that reader. I mark it failed because this specific omitted limitation bears directly on the independence that makes the technique attractive. This is not a finding that its five-fault account is false.

## What the tests leave open

The tests establish a minimum admission standard; they do not establish that this method improves decisions or merits its cost compared with a simpler review. A reader can find additional faults without improving the eventual decision, and counting faults can reward disputable objections. Here the offer explicitly acknowledges unchanged outcomes and cost, so the gap should not be mistaken for a hidden success claim.

Closed input withholds reasons, but does not by itself establish independence of judgment. A reader may share assumptions or training with the decider, inherit framing from the selected evidence and tests, or be affected by how the assignment is worded. A different provider is not proof that these effects disappear. No test requires examining those channels.

The author also being the decider adds control that an independent report alone cannot remove: the same role can select what to offer, write the admission tests, frame the reader's assignment, select the reader, interpret disagreements, and make the final decision. It can honestly record differences yet discount the ones most threatening to its own work. The tests require neither recusal nor another party's authority over the final disposition. Disclosure of self-verification helps the receiver assess this risk but does not remove it.

The tests also do not require checking whether every material dependency or stakeholder was represented in the closed input. A perfectly compliant read of an incomplete selection can miss the context that would reverse its judgment. This report's narrow evidence access is itself a limit: I can assess the offer and compare its permitted public citations, not independently reconstruct its claimed development and use.

Finally, the kit's timing can be read carefully or carelessly. The instruction to record the decider's own reading appears under “After the report,” but explicitly says “Before reading the report.” That is not a contradiction or a test failure. Moving it before issuing the assignment would reduce the chance that a user first sees the other reader's conclusions and then reconstructs an apparently independent initial judgment. The present tests do not directly check whether that precommitment actually happened.

## Corrections and disposition

Hold for a revised kit carrying the self-verification qualification and a dependency check before its removal/substitution instructions. The record need not claim stronger evidence or manufacture further uses. Optionally move the decider's initial-reading instruction earlier to make the intended order easier to follow. Refusal would exceed what these correctable defects warrant.
